## 1. Question

Which municipalities will miss the statutory household recycling target next year? A regional
waste authority has funding to send advisory teams to twenty municipalities a year, and
currently selects them by last year's rate alone. If a model can rank municipalities by
*risk* rather than by current level, the same twenty visits reach more of the councils that
will actually miss the target.

The decision is a shortlist of twenty from 600, so we report **precision@20** as the headline
alongside ROC-AUC, and we treat a missed at-risk municipality as roughly three times as
costly as a wasted visit. Accuracy is not reported: at a 33% base rate it would be dominated
by the majority class.

## 2. Data

Synthetic, 600 municipalities x 8 columns, one row per municipality-year (2025 reporting
year), generated by `src/data.py`. The generating process is documented in that file: recycling
performance depends on population density, deprivation, the number of collection points, and
a region effect, with realistic noise.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Rows | 600 (393 train regions / 207 test regions) |
| Features | 5 numeric, 2 categorical (12 regions as the group key) |
| Target | `missed_target` (1 = missed), base rate 0.33 |
| Missing | `collection_points` ~7%, `deprivation_index` ~4% |

Missing values are imputed with the median inside the pipeline, and a `was_missing`
indicator is kept: in this data, councils that fail to report collection points are
themselves more likely to miss the target, so the missingness is informative.

## 3. Method

**Split.** Municipalities are nested in regions, and regional policy is a shared cause, so a
random split would let the model memorise region-level effects and flatter itself.
We therefore use `GroupKFold(5)` grouped by `region` for tuning, and a grouped hold-out of
30% of regions for the final test. This is the paragraph the session-9 checklist asks for.

**Models.** A `LogisticRegression` inside a `Pipeline` (median imputation with indicator,
standardisation, one-hot for the two categorical columns) as the interpretable candidate, and
`HistGradientBoostingClassifier` on the same preprocessing as the flexible one. Baseline:
rank by last year's recycling rate, which is what the authority does today.

**Tuning.** `C` over `logspace(-2, 2, 9)` for the logistic model and `learning_rate` over
`[0.03, 0.1, 0.3]` for the booster, selected by mean grouped-CV ROC-AUC on the training
regions only. The test regions were scored once, after this report's method section was
written.

## 4. Results

Scored once on the four held-out regions (207 municipalities), after this section's method
was fixed.

| Model | ROC-AUC (test) | 95% bootstrap CI | precision@20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline: rank by current rate | 0.617 | [0.53, 0.69] | 0.60 |
| Logistic regression (pipeline, `C = 0.01`) | 0.773 | [0.70, 0.84] | 0.85 |
| Gradient boosting (`learning_rate = 0.03`) | 0.762 | [0.69, 0.82] | 0.55 |

The two models are indistinguishable on AUC - their intervals overlap almost entirely - so we
report the logistic regression, which is interpretable and cheaper to maintain. It improves
precision@20 over current practice from 12 of 20 to 17 of 20 visits reaching a municipality
that does miss the target: five more useful visits for the same budget.

Note that the booster has a comparable AUC and a *worse* precision@20 (11 of 20, below the
baseline). AUC averages over every threshold; the authority uses exactly one cut - the top
twenty - so the two metrics can disagree, and the one that matches the decision wins.

Disaggregating by population-density tercile, AUC is 0.807 [0.69, 0.90] for the largest,
0.791 [0.66, 0.90] for the smallest and 0.714 [0.59, 0.83] for the middle group. All three
intervals overlap heavily on 64-77 rows each, so the honest reading is that we cannot
distinguish subgroup performance at this sample size - not that the middle tercile is truly
worst. It is worth saying explicitly, because the intuitive expectation (small municipalities
predicted worst) is not what the data show.

## 5. Limitations

The data are synthetic, so every number above is a demonstration of method rather than a
finding about waste policy. Beyond that: the model is trained on reported figures, and
reporting quality is itself correlated with capacity, so a council that under-reports may be
scored as low-risk for the wrong reason - and because our missingness is deliberately
informative, the model partly learns *who does not report*. Eight training regions is few, so
the region effects are estimated poorly and transfer to an unlike region is untested. The
subgroup table is the result we are least confident in: 64-77 rows per group cannot separate
0.71 from 0.81.

## 6. Model card

- **Intended use:** ranking municipalities to allocate a fixed number of advisory visits, by a
  regional waste authority, once a year, with a human reviewing the shortlist.
- **Out-of-scope use:** any funding or penalty decision about an individual municipality;
  any use outside the region the training data cover; any use of the probability as a
  published risk figure.
- **Data:** synthetic (`src/data.py`, seed 2026), 600 municipality-years, notional 2025
  reporting year; ~7% and ~4% missingness in two columns; no personal data.
- **Performance:** test ROC-AUC 0.773 [0.70, 0.84]; precision@20 0.85 (17 of 20). Baseline
  0.617 and 0.60. By density tercile: 0.807 (large), 0.714 (medium), 0.791 (small), all
  intervals overlapping.
- **Threshold / decision rule:** no fixed threshold - take the top 20 by score, reviewed by a
  case officer. If a threshold is ever needed, use the 3:1 cost ratio from section 1 to set
  it, and re-derive it on fresh data.
- **Known limitations:** reporting-quality confounding; only eight training regions;
  subgroup differences not resolvable at this n; ranking is trustworthy, individual
  probabilities are not.
- **Monitoring:** compare the score distribution and the realised miss rate each reporting
  year; retrain annually; escalate if precision@20 falls below the 0.60 baseline.
